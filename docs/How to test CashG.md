# How to test CashG

Thank you for testing our project. CashG is a redesign concept for an e-wallet, made for our UI/UX course. It is **not** GCash and is not connected to it. Everything inside is sample data, and no real money moves.

It takes about 10 minutes.

## Start it

1. On the [CashG GitHub page](https://github.com/JayPing23/CashG), click **Code → Download ZIP** (or `git clone` it).
2. **Right-click the zip → Properties → tick "Unblock" → OK.** (This stops Windows from warning about a downloaded file.)
3. Right-click the zip → **Extract All**.
4. Open the extracted `CashG-main` folder and double-click **Start CashG**.
5. A black window opens and your browser shows CashG. Keep the black window open while you test. Close it when you are done.

The first start downloads a private copy of Node.js (about 36 MB, one time only), so it takes a minute. Later starts are instant.

If Windows still shows "Windows protected your PC", click **More info → Run anyway**.

Works on Windows 10 and 11. Nothing is installed on your computer.

**Stay connected to the internet and use Chrome or Edge.** With your OK (the app asks first), your clicks are recorded with Microsoft Clarity so our group can see where the design works. Brave and ad blockers stop the recording, so turn off Brave Shields or your ad blocker for `localhost` while testing.

## Please do not type real details

Do not enter a real MPIN, password, one-time code or bank detail anywhere. Made-up text is fine.

## Tasks

You will play two roles: first a **customer**, then a **support agent**, then the customer again. Say what you are thinking out loud if someone is observing.

### Part A: you are a customer

1. Look at the Home screen. Find out how much money is in the wallet.
2. You paid your electric bill and were charged twice. Find where to get help.
3. Send a support ticket about it.
4. Find and copy your ticket's reference number.
5. Open your ticket and find out what stage it is at.

### Part B: you are a support agent

6. Open **Profile → Support console** (on a laptop it is also at the bottom of the left menu).
7. Find the ticket you just sent.
8. Acknowledge it, then start the review.
9. Ask the customer for more information.

### Part C: you are the customer again

10. Go back to the customer app. Without being told where, find out that support needs something from you.
11. Reply to support.
12. Go to the Support console once more, resolve the ticket, then come back and read how it was resolved.

### Part D: look around

13. Open **All services**, **Send**, **Activity** and **Inbox**. These screens are design demonstrations only.
14. In **Profile**, switch between Light and Dark theme.
15. Tap the lightbulb at the top of any screen to read the design notes.

## After testing

Please answer the short questionnaire our group gives you. Honest answers help us most, including things you did not like.
